    How it works

    The eyes

    The input neurons are LC4 and LPLC2, the cells a fly uses to spot something about to hit it. The left eye watches the left half of the board and the right eye watches the right. As a block falls it gets "closer" and looms harder. The glow on the board shows what each eye is seeing.

    The brain

    240 middle-brain neurons are the strongest real links between those eyes and the fly's command neurons. Every weight is a real synapse count. Each neuron is a simple leaky integrate-and-fire model (from Shiu et al., 2024), stepped every 0.1 ms.

    The buttons

    Four groups of descending neurons, which carry commands from brain to body, are wired to the controller. Turn-left neurons press left, turn-right neurons press right, walk-forward neurons rotate, and escape neurons drop. Whichever group is most unusually active right now presses its button.

    This is a toy, not a science result. The wiring is real, but the mapping from board to eyes and from neurons to buttons is my design. A real fly would just fly away.
